## 01 · Playful

**Estado:** ✅ Funciona bien.

```text
Fast playful lo-fi female-fronted punk, around 185–190 BPM.

The core of the song is an original naïve, bouncy sing-song vocal melody: simple, repetitive and almost childlike in shape, like a playground melody reimagined by a scrappy punk band. It should feel playful, slightly awkward and instantly memorable, never sugary or polished.

Guitar-led arrangement: blunt dry distorted rhythm chords, gritty simple bass and jittery live drums with a slight bounce. A second guitar may add tiny clipped melodic answers between vocal phrases.

Add only a VERY sparse soft synthetic mallet sound: muted, dry, rounded and low in the mix. Use isolated single notes or tiny two-note accents only in occasional gaps between phrases. Never create a mallet riff, ostinato, solo, instrumental section or continuous melody. No bright xylophone or glockenspiel tone.

The song should be strongly vocal-driven. Start singing almost immediately. Lead vocals should occupy nearly the entire song, with instrumental gaps usually no longer than one or two bars. No extended intro, interlude, solo or outro.

Adult female lead, close and forward in the mix. The voice is playful, melodic and imperfect rather than powerful. Use a slightly high, light register without becoming piercing.

Let the singer play with the melody:
- sudden but musical pitch jumps
- tiny cracks and unstable notes
- rushed clusters of syllables
- occasional delayed or lazy syllables
- abrupt phrase endings
- short half-spoken fragments
- small rhythmic mouth sounds such as “tch!”, “pff!”, “mm-mm”, “ha!”
- brief rough raspy attacks on isolated words

Keep those vocal quirks short and integrated into the singing. No sustained screams, shrieks, guttural vocals or harsh high-pitched yelling. Most of the performance must remain clearly sung and melodic.

Keep a continuous forward pulse. Allow a few tiny one-beat interruptions or false starts for playful tension, followed immediately by the band returning. Do not use long silences, breakdowns or dramatic stop-start structures.

Verses can mix longer flowing phrases with short answers, so the vocal density changes naturally while the band keeps moving. The chorus should be very simple, repetitive and catchy, with a naïve melodic hook rather than a huge rock anthem.

Lo-fi, dry, narrow rehearsal-room production. Small imperfections are welcome.

No glossy pop-punk, emo, polished indie-pop, EDM, trap, cinematic build-ups, guitar solos, long instrumental passages, bright toy instruments or layered choir vocals.
```

### Qué funcionó

- Melodía naïf y muy pegadiza sobre una base punk seca.
- Voz femenina adulta, imperfecta y juguetona.
- Rarezas vocales breves sin convertir toda la interpretación en fraseo atropellado.
- Mucha presencia vocal y pocos huecos instrumentales.

## 02 · Toy Punk

**Estado:** ✅ Funciona bien.

```text
Fast playful female-fronted toy punk / lo-fi garage punk, around 175–185 BPM.

Raw, dry and energetic small-band sound: crunchy rhythm guitar, simple bouncy bass and tight live drums with a slightly nervous pulse.

Build the song around an original naïve, playful, sing-song vocal melody. The melodic shapes should be very simple, repetitive, slightly awkward and instantly catchy, almost like a playground chant transplanted into a scrappy adult punk song.

The lyrics and subject matter must clearly be adult. The childlike quality applies ONLY to the melody, never to the lyrics or vocal age.

Adult female lead vocal, close and very forward in the mix. Playful, imperfect and expressive, with little pitch cracks, sudden melodic jumps, lazy syllables, clipped endings and occasional brief raspy attacks.

IMPORTANT VOCAL BALANCE:
Most of the song should use clear, catchy melodic singing.

Use rushed, densely packed phrasing only occasionally, mainly in selected parts of the verses. Alternate one longer tumbling melodic sentence with several shorter, cleaner sung lines.

Do not make every verse fast or wordy.

The chorus should completely simplify into a short, highly melodic and repetitive hook with plenty of space between phrases. No rushed vocal phrasing in the main chorus.

Add occasional tiny vocal reactions such as “tch!”, “pff!”, “mm-mm”, “ha!” or short muttered fragments between sung lines.

Use soft synthetic toy-mallet or muted xylophone accents as part of the arrangement. They should be clearly audible but secondary: isolated single notes, tiny two- or three-note answers and short accents that answer the vocal melody.

Never use mallets as a solo instrument, continuous riff, long pattern or instrumental section.

Keep vocals present through most of the track. Start singing almost immediately. Instrumental gaps should normally be no longer than one or two bars.

Allow a few very short one-beat stops or false starts for nervous energy, but keep the song flowing continuously.

The overall feeling should be catchy, playful, slightly awkward, sarcastic and rough around the edges.

No glossy pop-punk, no emo, no children's-music lyrics, no cute child voice, no long instrumental passages, no guitar solos, no extended mallet sections, no constant shouting and no polished commercial production.
```

### Qué funcionó

- Toy punk rápido y claramente melódico.
- Buen equilibrio entre fraseo atropellado puntual y partes pegadizas.
- Estribillo más sencillo y melódico que los versos.
- Mallets/xilófono como acento, no como protagonista.

## Skill 02 · Alternative / grunge / bubblegrunge

**Estado:** ✅ Validado provisionalmente.

**Resultado de prueba:** una de las dos generaciones funcionó bien. Mantener este prompt como base para nuevas pruebas.

```text
Fast female-fronted bubblegrunge / alternative rock, around 185–195 BPM.

THE ENTIRE SONG MUST BE SUNG IN SPANISH.

Use natural contemporary Spanish from Spain, with conversational adult lyrics that feel originally written in Spanish rather than translated from English.

The language should sound informal, spontaneous and lived-in rather than literary or overly polished.

Occasionally use natural colloquial reductions, contractions, shortened pronunciations, slang, vulgar expressions or profanity when they fit the character, situation and rhythm of the line.

Use them sparingly and unpredictably. Do not turn colloquial language or vulgarity into a gimmick, and do not associate it automatically with anger or aggression.

The subject matter can be funny, intimate, awkward, affectionate, bored, frustrated, excited, uncomfortable or completely mundane.

Musically, use thick fuzzy rhythm guitars over a thinner jangly clean-guitar layer, a simple tuneful bass and urgent dry live drums.

Keep the track fast, energetic and guitar-driven.

Verses should feel tight, brittle and slightly restrained, with the guitars leaving enough room for the vocal melody.

The chorus should open up into a bigger, fuzzier and more abrasive sound while remaining strongly melodic and catchy.

Adult female lead vocal, close and forward in the mix.

The voice should be melodic and expressive but rough around the edges:
small cracks,
brief rasp,
slightly unstable notes,
occasional strained higher notes,
and short bursts of more aggressive singing when the arrangement opens up.

Most of the performance should remain clearly sung and melodic.

Use memorable vocal hooks with simple shapes and strong rhythm. Occasional longer or faster phrases are welcome, but do not make the whole song rushed or wordy.

Allow one brief moment where the arrangement drops mostly to bass and voice before the full band returns sharply.

Keep vocals present through most of the track and avoid long instrumental passages.

Raw narrow cassette-demo production, dry drums, fuzzy guitars and small imperfections.

No glossy pop-punk, no emo melodrama, no polished indie-pop, no metal riffing, no synths, no toy instruments, no guitar solos and no long instrumental intro or outro.
```
